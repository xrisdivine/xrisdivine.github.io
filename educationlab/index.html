Why this exists 👀

This is a small playground with a big claim: the first prompting skill isn't typing — it's describing. A kid learns that vague words get a vague result, and careful, specific words get exactly what they pictured. No lecture, no tutorial. They just notice that better words make Sprout do cooler things, and they chase that.

capability, not engagement no tutorial — implicit learning quality made visible scaffold that fades

The one design idea

Prompting quality is invisible, so beginners can't tell a good prompt from a lazy one. The Describing Power meter makes it visible: each kind of detail a child adds — a doing word, a size, a speed, a count, a feeling, a color — lights up and Sprout's response gets richer in the exact dimension they described. The feedback loop is the lesson.

Maps to the 4D AI-fluency frame

Description

The whole point: specifying intent precisely enough to get what you want. This is that muscle, at age six.

Discernment

The kid judges Sprout's result against what they pictured — and revises. Noticing the gap is the skill.

Delegation

You hand a task to a capable helper. Loops (next tab) extend this to "do it many times for me."

Diligence

Try, look closely, adjust, try again. The meter rewards care over speed.

Choices I'd defend

  • Deterministic engine, no LLM. Instant, offline, free, and safe for kids — and it let me author the exact word→behavior mapping the lesson depends on. An API call can't guarantee the pedagogy.
  • The idea drawer is optional scaffolding. Word chips lower the floor for pre-readers, then get out of the way once a child types their own.
  • Reward describing, never punish. A one-word prompt still works — Sprout just asks a friendly question about the one detail that would help most. Productive nudge, not an error state.
  • Accessibility from the start: full keyboard use, labeled controls, an aria-live voice for Sprout, and a real prefers-reduced-motion path.

How you'd measure it

Describing Power per prompt over a session (does it climb?), unique detail-dimensions used, revision rate after a low-detail try, and a withdrawal test — hide the meter and the idea drawer, and see if richer prompts persist. Capability that survives the scaffold coming off is the only score that counts.
