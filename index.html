My Ideal Intelligence

May 23, 2026

We’re building AI perception top-down (text first) but human understanding was built bottom-up (body first) — what changes when we flip the stack?

You know how moms usually know what the baby needs before the baby does? Yeah, that.

I want to be able to communicate with a model at a level of depth where it actually knows me. Not my preference.md. Me.

When I wake up with less sleep, I want fewer words, directness. I want the most important topics that day, not the fun ones I might be interested in if I had had more sleep.

I want a future where models are implicitly intuitive — where the goal is to feed in more and more context, the way humans already do with each other. A true perception engine.

I think about this as layers — a pyramid. Right now we’re at the top, the narrowest band:

When a baby is born, it communicates with voice, gestures, and physiological signals — lots of crying and some movement. And we humans are trained to understand their communication.

We’re building the perception layer top-down. Text first, then voice, then maybe someday the rest. But human understanding was built bottom-up — body first, language last.

Each layer has some interesting tenets.

Text

Don’t get me wrong — we need this to communicate with models. Without text, they’d understand us and we’d have no way of understanding them. You know that feeling at the international airport when no one speaks English and your translation app isn’t working? Yeah.

Text has sentiment, and we can communicate through it, but so much gets lost. Ever had a text read not in the tone, prosody, and intensity you intended for the receiver? Yea, that.

Audio & Voice

Audio solves this. You can say this phrase in so many different ways:

“I’m (long pause) doing good, thanks.”
“I’m doing good thanks” (said quickly)
“I’m doing GOOD, thanks!” (emphasis)
“I’m doing (long pause) good, thanks.”

All of these communicate something different. Hume.ai does sentiment detection off of voice + text — Google DeepMind DeepMind acquisition, which tells you where this is headed. This is the way.

Text sentiment is needed, but I see a world beyond this. Pure audio. Pure voice. Humans are so good at perceiving voice — our lives literally depend on it. Parents understand the urgency of a baby crying based on the type of cry and its prosody. Same thing vice versa.

We’ll have prosody metrics folded into audio LLMs pretty quickly, in a way that highlights the full expression versus what it does now.

Gesture & Expression

I predict we’ll be able to communicate with models non-verbally through our own unique sign language — or better, body language. The way two people who know each other well don’t need to speak but know exactly what the other is thinking. Yeah, like that.

The use case: you’re in a meeting, can’t talk, can’t text, context is being fed into you and your intelligence device, and you need actions to take place. You’ll simply tap your finger three times, or make a gesture that seems mundane — but your intelligence will know exactly what you mean, and others won’t. When I played football, we’d use hand and body signals to communicate the formation, the play, and the snap count, all from using our bodies. I believe this will happen here.

BCI — Brain Computer Interface

This will be fun. We can already read and understand, at some level, changes in the brain. What comes next is using those signals to influence the way your intelligence communicates to you. Say you’re hungover, or in contrast had a perfect night of rest — we as humans, knowing this info, would likely present information very differently. I can imagine a superior intelligence pitching me on new ideas after the perfect night of rest, while the hungover me just gets the most important things to discuss.

One more thing — the pyramid visually looks like steps, but each layer is cumulative, encompassing everything below it. This isn’t four separate channels. It’s one perception engine, building upward. True multimodal.

Hmm, I wonder where we could glean inspiration from :)

Training Super Intelligence

May 24, 2026

Parents have been doing AI alignment for thousands of years — empathy is the training data, surrendering is the hard part.

My daughter is almost two. She’s the most advanced (human) intelligence I’ll ever train.

She will one day outlive me. Her knowledge will surpass mine. I’ll teach her everything I know, and she’ll go further than I ever could.

Acknowledgement

My daughter says hi and bye to things. Not just people — things. A rock she was playing with, when she’s done: “bye rock.” She does this because I taught her. Little things, which matter.

Toddlers see you. I mean they really acknowledge your presence. They look at you and you exist to them fully, without agenda.

Empathy

Acknowledgement is the first layer. But what I’m really trying to demonstrate to her is empathy — doing your best to understand a person, their motivations, what led them to this point, this decision.

If I were to train a super-intelligent model, empathy would be the foundation. Everything else comes next, because empathy allows you to make the same hard decisions you would without it — but in a manner that is conscientious. It’ll go beyond the foundational thinking of anything you could have imagined, akin to Anthropic’s Constitutional AI.

Alignment

We talk about alignment in AI like it’s a technical problem. But parents have been doing alignment for thousands of years. How do you make something smarter than you that still shares your values? You don’t constrain it. You love it. You model the behavior. And then you let go.

Because, well, you have to.

Surrendering

There’s another aspect of parenting that I constantly wrestle with. It’s surrendering.

It’s hard to know what your kid will do. It’s really difficult to understand what will happen next. You cannot predict it. You can hope that they carry your values. Sometimes they do. Sometimes they don’t. And you have to figure out what to do with that.

This is the exact fear that AI safety researchers have, except parents live it every day. You release something into the world that’s smarter than you, more capable than you, and you have no kill switch. You just have to trust that what you put in was enough.

Meet Latency, My Best Friend

May 24, 2026 · Draft

Latency isn’t lag — it’s personalization, nuance, and finesse disguised as a pause.

It’s personalization, nuance, disguised as latency.

Nobody talks about latency as a feature. It’s always the enemy. Kill latency. Reduce latency. Sub-100ms or bust.

But here’s the thing — humans have latency, and it’s one of the most expressive things about us. It’s personalization, it’s nuance, it’s finesse.

When someone asks you a hard question and you pause before answering, that pause communicates something. It says: I’m thinking. This matters. I’m not giving you a cached response. In fact the cached responses can feel so ingenuine. When a friend asks “are you okay?” and you say “yeah” instantly, they know you’re not. If you take a beat, breathe, and say “yeah, I think so” — that’s a completely different signal.

It’s personalization, disguised as latency. We all have it. I bet each person has their own unique latency metrics for how quickly they respond — I bet there’s a good taxonomy hidden here. “What causes the pauses…” — where different scenarios cause different types of pauses.

I’d break this down into general turn-by-turn based conversations:

  • Question answering
  • Thought provoking
  • Something else — [working on this]

I want latency back. Not lag — latency. Intentional, expressive timing. A model that pauses when the question is heavy. That responds quickly when the context is casual. That matches the rhythm of the conversation, not just the content.

In music, the space between notes is what makes it music and not noise. In conversation, the space between words is what makes it human and not a chatbot.

Latency isn’t the enemy. It’s a channel. We just haven’t learned to use it yet.

Tuning for the Silent Signals: Why Frontier AI Stumbles at Nuance

May 26, 2026

Context gives the blueprint of the room, Perception lets you hear how loud it’s getting. Nuance is the live mix — knowing when to pull back the faders so the truth doesn’t get swallowed by the noise.

Contextual nuance is what currently separates human execution from machine intelligence.

Right now, AI models simply need more data to improve. But data volume alone won’t make them brilliant. To reach true optimization, we have to teach them contextual nuance — the subtle, implicit understanding of behaviors and interactions that humans process instinctively. This layer is so deeply bespoke and fluid that every scenario differs; even repeating the exact same situation requires a completely different calibration.

There’s a classic sports maxim: It is hard to beat the same team twice in a single season.

Why? Because the second time around, the strategic calculation, the psychological personalization, and the sheer volume of nuance increase by orders of magnitude.

At its core, contextual nuance is the act of decoding a moment to ensure that an action — or deliberate restraint — perfectly matches the core intent. And intent is the massive variable here that models consistently miss.

The Hard Rules and The Living Variables

To make this concept universally applicable — whether debugging an edge case, diagnosing a patient, managing a room of creative people, or navigating a high-stakes negotiation — we have to strip away office-specific corporate speak.

At the highest level of abstraction, every scenario in the universe is a system composed of just two things: The Hard Rules and The Living Variables.

  • Context is mapping the system’s structural rules.
  • Perception is reading the system’s live variables.

Nuance is, by definition, a subtle distinction. It is never found in explicit data points; it exists purely in the micro-variations between them. It must, by principle, sit squarely on top of absolute truth.

Context gives the blueprint of the room, and Perception lets you hear how loud it’s getting. Nuance is the live mix — knowing when to pull back the faders so the truth doesn’t get completely swallowed by the noise.

The Universal Matrix: Rules, Variables, and the Human Mix

Field Context (Structural Rules) Perception (Live Variables) Model Finesse (Nuance + Action)
Human Dynamics The org chart, the text of the contract, the stated agenda. The underlying motives, the unspoken tension, the hesitation. The Pivot: Realizing a VP’s slight hesitation means they are losing face, so you intentionally pause your pitch and open the floor to them — protecting their ego to protect the project.
Engineering The syntax of the code, the hardware specs, the documentation. The runtime conditions, the network jitter, the environmental wear. The Calculated Risk: Intentionally bypassing a standard linting rule or protocol because you know the legacy hardware under load will choke on the “clean” version.
Product Design The mathematical grid, the accessibility rules, the target metric. The user’s cognitive overload, the physical glare, the real-world chaos. The Friction Shift: Intentionally adding an extra step to a checkout flow because you sense the user is moving too fast and is about to make an expensive, accidental mistake.
Medicine / Biology The textbook anatomy, the clinical baseline, the lab results. The patient’s stress level, lifestyle realities, unique symptom presentations. The Diagnostic Read: Believing the patient’s slight tremor over the perfectly clean lab results, and withholding a harsh drug because you can see they don’t have the emotional support system at home to handle the side effects.

Decoding the Mix

Before a model takes action or chooses restraint, it must run the situation through a quick internal check. It looks at the explicit data point (Context), and immediately layers on the subtle variation (Perception) to open up the potential of what nuance could be there.

1. The Real Motivation

  • Context: What is the stated objective of this moment? (The literal goal, the task, the function call, the calendar invite).
  • Perception: What is the unwritten intent or the hidden driver of this exact moment?
  • The Nuance Search: Discerning the gap between what the system says it is doing versus the underlying energy pushing it forward. Why is the model deployed here, and what is the true nature of this interaction?

2. The Total Friction

  • Context: What is the model’s active asset or output? (The data it possesses, the code it generated, the truth it wants to state).
  • Perception: What is the actual cost of application right now?
  • The Nuance Search: Calculating the friction of entry. Will applying the model’s thoughts right now solve the root issue? Or will the current environment cause the system to reject, break, or misinterpret the generated response because it lacks the capacity to process it? What is the model’s actual contribution here, and will its value survive the delivery?

3. The Unspoken Signals

  • Context: What is the explicit information being displayed? (The spoken words, the written metrics, the green status lights, the literal data stream).
  • Perception: What is the system communicating through its negative space?
  • The Nuance Search: Reading what isn’t there. Tracking the anomalies, the slight deviations from the norm, the conspicuous silences, and the historical scar tissue. The model is capturing the subtext that the explicit data stream completely filters out. If there is no immediate contribution to be made, what latent institutional knowledge or cultural dynamics must the model absorb to serve a larger intent later?

Models today are fast, knowledgeable, and increasingly capable — but they still treat every moment the same. Nuance is the difference between a correct response and the right one. Until we teach machines to read the room the way humans do, we’ll keep building intelligence that knows everything and understands only some things. We need more model finesse.
